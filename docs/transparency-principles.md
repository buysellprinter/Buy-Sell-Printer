# BSP Transparency Principles

BuySellPrinter's intended brand promise is grounded in clear communication. High-value used-equipment decisions should not depend on vague claims alone.

## 1. Separate claims from checked information

Information supplied by a seller should be identified as such. Information independently checked should describe what was checked and what evidence was used.

## 2. Be specific about verification

A word such as “verified” should never imply that every aspect of a machine has been checked. Verification should identify its scope, date and limitations.

## 3. Describe observations honestly

If an inspection is available, its findings should describe the machine as observed at that time. The report should not imply that all internal components have been assessed unless that work was actually performed.

## 4. Communicate known issues

Material known issues should be disclosed in a clear, understandable way. Unknown information should not be presented as confirmed.

## 5. Make transaction terms understandable

Machine price, applicable platform fees, logistics and other costs should be explained before a user commits, whenever BSP is responsible for presenting or coordinating those terms.

## 6. Keep expectations realistic

A used or refurbished machine may require future maintenance or repair. An inspection can reduce uncertainty but cannot remove all risk.

## 7. Improve the process over time

Clear documentation and consistent communication can help buyers and sellers make better-informed decisions.

These principles describe BSP's intended approach. Specific services and verification practices will depend on the capabilities actually offered for a given listing or transaction.
